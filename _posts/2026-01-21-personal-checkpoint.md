## The Start of My Career

I have been an engineer for about 8 years now.

The very beginning captured in this [venture](./2018-05-23-fourthstate-plasma.md). It is typical to consider the first job out of college as the "start" but I was in the middle of junior year at UC Berkely when starting this project. All I knew at the time is what I was interested in and luckily stumbled upon people and cyperpunk environment, Ethereum, that had a pressing need for new technology. The perfect recipe to make things happen. Looking back on this, I feel strongly about this for a couple of reasons

1. Money. We were the one of the very teams to drive a technical solution in this problem space. As a result all the research grants we applied for were given to us with almost no pushback. ~$200k across both Ethereum Foundation and the Ethereum Community Fund. It was the first time in my life I dealt with money in this size, which quickly turned into responsibility and delivering something to show for (which we did).

2. I was not alone. I worked on this project with 3 good friends at the time and we split the money evenly. It became a crash course on how to work effectively as a team (i.e technical process) and the unavoidable social dynamics that becomes the root of tension. The project was not started with the profit-based motives, thus it was natural to split things evenly. What happens when contributions are not equal, what becomes fair? This is not a prelude to any particular story, but general dynamics everyone faces in the real-world.

3. Traveling the world. The Ethereum ecosystem in ~2018 was young and global. It was also democratic -- anyone interested and motivated could do impactful work. It was also an industry with a lot of money, though the many ICOs of cryptocurrencies. Having expertise in a growing industry can quickly make you a technical leader. It was strange as a 20 year old attendeding international conferences to speak, collaborate with companies, let alone be seriously asked for my opinion on anything.

4. Delivering. It is easy to talk a big game about what you're going to do. Very hard to execute. I'm not even sure if I would've bet on myself from the outside looking in. No one on our team had any real engineering experience besides the few school projects in classes, nor any mentorship. The best way to learn is to just do the thing which is how we approached it. And by the end we had an implementation highlighted as ecosystem reference and forked by many companies. Open sourced libraries used till this day (~100 weekly downloads) noted in technical literature.
